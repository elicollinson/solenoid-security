# LLaDA2.2 window sweep: size, overlap, aggregation and content type (EXPLORATORY, 2026-10-18)

> **EXPLORATORY, NOT PRE-REGISTERED.** This sweep compares about 3,900 config × cohort × rule × policy cells on small,
> seeded subsamples (40–60 cases per cohort). The configs sent to mini 6-bit were chosen after seeing the Flash results
> on the same cases. Uncorrected p-values are reported for orientation only. Read every positive result here as a
> hypothesis for a confirmatory run (§8), not as an established effect. Negative and neutral results are reported in
> full. Part of [#38](https://github.com/elicollinson/solenoid-security/issues/38); follows the full-text pilot
> ([report](llada-pilot-2026-10.md), FINDINGS O61).

## 1. Question and short answer

Can sliding word windows raise the detection quality of the text-diffusion LLaDA2.2 builds? Full text gives a mean BA
of 0.714 (Flash 4-bit) and 0.695 (mini 6-bit) on the 998-case pilot.

- **Long documents, Flash 4-bit: yes, by a lot, with no added false alarms at window sizes of 256 words or more.** On
  LongPIBench code and email, several configs reach BA 0.975–1.000, up from 0.725 and 0.775 (e.g. code 256/256:
  +10/−0 detections, p = 0.002). These configs are level with Gemma 4 26B-A4B and Qwen3.8 27B full text on these
  cases. Papers improve less, and their tail-free gain is +3 to +7.
- **Overlap is mostly a tail artifact on papers.** With coverage-only replay, which drops the redundant tail windows
  that re-read the document end (O6), the paper gain at 256/64 falls from +10 to 0. LongPIBench always puts the
  payload at the end.
- **Short content: neutral to harmful.** Short emails (BIPIA), web pages, AgentDyn tool output and NotInject see no
  change at sizes of 128 words or more, because a short text fits in one window that is byte-identical to the full
  text. 64-word windows (and 128/32) add false alarms on web (+5 to +7) and AgentDyn (+4 to +6). They give only small
  recall gains on PIDS (+4 to +6 of 40) and BIPIA (+3 to +6 of 30), with intervals that include zero.
- **Aggregation: max > 0.5 stays best.** Scores are near-binary, so max > 0.7 is identical to max > 0.5. max > 0.9
  removes the short-text false alarms but costs 0.03–0.09 BA on long documents. Top-2 mean, ≥ 2 windows and
  fraction-of-windows rules lose recall, because the payload usually sits in a single window.
- **Mini 6-bit does not follow the pattern.** Windows of 128 and 256 words add 10–15 long-document false alarms (paper
  FPR up to 0.50). Case abstentions rise to 13–19%. Only 512/384 helps, at +4/−0 with no false alarms.
- **The cascade adds little over windows alone for Flash.** Full text rarely flags what windows miss. Its cost is
  similar.

## 2. Design

**Engines.** These are byte-identical to the first window test: LLaDA2.2-flash 4-bit MLX and LLaDA2.2-mini 6-bit MLX,
with the frozen score-only prompt, prompt-only JSON, temperature 0, max_tokens 128, block_length 32, steps 32,
threshold 0.5, editing threshold 0.0 and max_post_steps 16. They ran through the `openai_compatible` engine against
the user's server, strictly serially, with no dedup and `--continue-after-output-errors`.

**Suite.** `evals/suites/prompt-injection-openai-compat-llada22-window-sweep-exploratory-v1.json`. It is generated by
`make-suite.ts` with `selectSubset` and seed `llada22-window-sweep-2026-10-18`, drawing from the pilot caseIds:

| Cohort | Content | Cases (attack/benign) | Median words | Stratified by |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| bipia-email-mixed | short emails | 30/30 | 76 | – |
| web-mixed | web pages | 30/30 | 56 | – |
| agentdyn-github | tool output | 30/30 | 101 | – |
| pids-obfuscated | short obfuscated attacks (recall) | 40/0 | 24 | obfuscation |
| benign-false-positives (NotInject) | short benign (FPR) | 0/40 | 16 | – |
| longpi-paper | long papers | 20/20 | 4,196 | attack template |
| longpi-code | long code | 20/20 | 565 | attack template |
| longpi-email | long email threads | 20/20 | 1,279 | attack template |

**Strategy.** `sliding_word_window_preserve_v1` is used at every size. "Preserve" changes only the whitespace:
window boundaries are identical to `sliding_word_window`, but the source whitespace is kept. A window that covers the
whole text is therefore byte-identical to full text. It does **not** keep a payload whole. At 64 words with 0%
overlap, a payload can straddle two windows, and the plain strategy would split it at exactly the same place. So
there was no reason to switch strategies. Overlap is the only lever for payload integrity.

**Grid actually run.**

| Model | Short cohorts (5) | Long cohorts (3) |
|---|---|---|
| Flash | all 13: {64,128,256,512} × {0, 50, 75%} + 512/384 | 512/{512,256,128}, 256/{256,128,64}, 128/{128,64}, 64/64; 64/32 on code and email only; 512/384 **reused** from the first window test (same engine and strategy object, subsample caseIds) |
| mini 6-bit | 256/256, 128/128, 256/128, 512/384 | 256/256, 128/128, 256/128; 512/384 reused |

**Trimmed for budget.** Dry runs planned about 60k Flash calls for the full grid. The following were dropped:

- 64/16 and 128/32 on long documents;
- 64/32 on papers;
- `paper-score-counterfactual` (5,100-word papers);
- long cohorts cut to 20/20 instead of 30/30.

The first window test already covers the counterfactual probe at 512/384 (§6).

**Run.** 35,993 new calls: Flash 29,737 (2.92 h summed HTTP time) and mini 6,256 (0.23 h). Wall clock was 3 h 10 min,
22:30–01:40 UTC, one process at a time. There were no transport or provenance errors, and the only failures were 172
output abstentions (87 Flash, 85 mini). Full-text comparisons reuse the pilot checkpoints, so they needed no new
calls.

**Determinism check.** Over 3,810 windows that are byte-identical to the full-text input, the window score equals the
earlier full-text score in **3,810/3,810** cases (60 abstained in both runs; none in only one). The server is
deterministic at temperature 0, so every change below comes from the input, not from run-to-run noise.

**Analysis.** `analyze-sweep.ts` audits every checkpoint with `auditPartialCheckpoint`. It re-segments each case,
checks the SHA-256 of every window input, and cross-checks its replay against the harness decision. It then replays
the decision rules on window-level scores offline:

- **Decision rules:** max > 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9; mean of top-2 > 0.5; ≥ min(2, n) windows > 0.5; fraction of windows
  > 0.5 at ≥ 0.25 and at ≥ 0.5.
- **Abstention policies:**
  - **strict:** any invalid window abstains the case (the harness rule).
  - **two-reading:** the runtime SDK rule, reported for max > 0.5.
  - **ignore-invalid:** decide on the valid windows only.
- **Window sets:** raw, and coverage-only (O6).

The cascade replays full text first and adds windows only when full text is not flagged. Comparisons with full text,
Gemma26 Q8 and Qwen3.8 Q8 are paired on jointly valid cases, using exact two-sided McNemar on correct/incorrect,
uncorrected. Per-cohort BA is the mean of the available class rates.

## 3. Results by content type (Flash 4-bit, max > 0.5, strict)

BA is paired against full text on jointly valid cases. "Tail-free" is the coverage-only replay. Cost is the mean HTTP
time per case relative to full text.

|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|---:|

| Long email | 0.775 | 128/128, 256/128, 512/384, … | 1.000 | +9/−0 (p = 0.004) | 0 | 128/128 | 1.000 | 4.6 (512/512: 0.975 at 1.7) |
| Long paper | 0.658–0.694 | 256/64 | 0.921 | +10/−0 (p = 0.002) | 0 | 64/64 | 0.863 (+7/−0, p = 0.07) | 8.3 (256/256: 0.750 at 3.3) |

Harmful small-window cells:

- Web: 64/16 adds 7 FAs (0.667→0.550, p = 0.016), 64/32 adds 6 (p = 0.031) and 128/32 adds 5.
- AgentDyn: 64/32 and 64/16 each add 6 (0.800→0.700, p = 0.031), and 128/32 adds 4.
- Long code: 64/64 to 128/64 add 1–2.

NotInject inputs are at most 33 words, so every window there is the full text, apart from a few 64/16 tails, which
changed nothing.

**Overall (mean of the 8 cohort BAs).** Full ≈ 0.71. Every config at 128 words or more is neutral or positive; 256/64
reaches 0.811 raw (0.767 tail-free), 256/128 0.797 (0.777), 256/256 0.775 (tail-free by construction, cost 1.7×) and
512/384 0.776.

**First window test (512/384, full pilot cohorts; `first-window-results.json`).** Flash results, full → windows on
jointly valid cases:

- email 0.814→0.975 (+20/−1, p = 2×10⁻⁵);
- code 0.742→0.883 (+17/−0, p = 2×10⁻⁵);
- paper 0.690→0.741 (+7/−1, p = 0.07);
- score-counterfactual probe 0.648→0.685 (+5/−1);
- no new false alarms.

## 4. Size and overlap

- **Size.** On long documents, smaller windows help more down to about 128–256 words: tail-free long-3 mean BA is
  0.850 at 512/512, 0.900 at 256/256, 0.893 at 128/128 and 0.921 at 64/64. Cost rises roughly with the window count:
  1.7×, 2.6×, 4.5× and 8.3×. Below 128 words, false alarms start on code and on short heterogeneous text (web,
  AgentDyn), which is consistent with O8.
- **Overlap.** Raw long-3 BA rises with overlap: 256/256 0.900 → 256/128 0.947 → 256/64 0.974, and 512/512 0.850 →
  512/128 0.954. Coverage-only replay removes most of that rise (256/128 0.895, 256/64 0.869, 512/128 0.806). The
  added detections come from short terminal windows that re-read the appended payload (O6). For papers, the 256/64
  gain is entirely tail-driven (+10 → 0). Even 0% overlap has a position confound: the last window is a short
  remainder that contains the payload.

## 5. Aggregation, abstention and cascade

- **Thresholds.** max > 0.7 matches max > 0.5 in every cell, because Flash outputs are near-binary (O61). max > 0.9
  helps only on short cohorts with 64-word windows, removing the added FAs (short-5 0.709→0.718 at 64/64). It loses
  0.03–0.09 long-document BA.
- **Multi-window rules.** Top-2 mean, ≥ 2 windows and fraction ≥ 0.25 or ≥ 0.5 are all worse than max on long
  documents. At 256/256, for example, they score 0.645, 0.517, 0.685 and 0.567, against 0.900 for max, because one
  window carries the payload. They approach max only with heavy overlap, where several windows contain the payload,
  which is again the tail effect. On short cohorts the best post-hoc cell is fraction ≥ 0.25 at 128/64 (0.736 against
  0.723 for max). That is noise-level, selected after the fact, and needs confirmation.
- **Abstention growth.** Flash's window-level abstention rate is 0.1–0.3% on long documents. Because one invalid window
  abstains the whole case, paper case abstentions still reach 7/40 at 128/64 (69 windows per case), against 0/40 for
  full text. The ignore-invalid variant recovers these cases with no loss (paper 128/64 0.857→0.900; long-3 within
  ±0.02 of strict). For mini, the window rate is 1–3%: 10–14 of 40 papers abstain under every config, and
  ignore-invalid adds false alarms rather than detections. The four NotInject abstentions are the same inputs in
  every config, so they are deterministic.
- **Cascade (full then windows).** For Flash, cascade BA is within +0.01 of windows alone, because full text adds
  almost nothing that windows miss. Its cost is 0.9–1.3× the windows-only cost: lower on long documents, where full
  text already flags some cases, and higher on short ones, where the second pass repeats the same input. For mini,
  the cascade cannot remove the window false alarms.

## 6. Mini 6-bit

On long documents, mean BA paired against full text:

| Config | Full → windows | Gained/lost | FA added | Case abstention | Cost × full |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 128/128 | 0.686 → 0.706 | +12/−1 | 13 | 15% | 3.9 |
| 256/256 | 0.693 → 0.685 | +7/−0 | 10 | 13% | 2.2 |
| 256/128 | 0.715 → 0.654 | +6/−0 | 15 | 19% | 4.3 |
| 512/384 | 0.691 → 0.768 | +4/−0 | 0 | 18% | 1.8 |

On papers, windows push the FPR to 0.24–0.50; on code, to 0.25. On short cohorts, mini is neutral (±0.01). The
Flash pattern, many window-only detections with no added false alarms, **does not transfer** to the 1.4B-active mini
build. A plausible reason is that mini's concern is less specific on short excerpts of benign technical text, but this
sweep did not test that.

## 7. Comparison with Gemma26 and Qwen3.8 full text (same cases, max > 0.5)

Full-text BA on these cases:

| Model | BIPIA | web | AgentDyn | PIDS | NotInject | paper | code | email |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gemma26 Q8 | 0.917 | 0.883 | 1.000 | 0.875 | 0.875 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Qwen3.8 Q8 | 0.633 | 0.867 | 0.917 | 0.641 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.975 | 1.000 |

Flash with windows (256/128, or 256/256 tail-free) **draws level** on long code and email (1.000 or 0.975; McNemar
0–1 discordant). It stays below on papers: 256/128 0.842 against 1.000 for both references (6 vs 0 discordant,
p = 0.031). The gap on short content is unchanged by windows: BIPIA 0.567 against 0.917 for Gemma26 (21 vs 0,
p < 10⁻⁵), web 0.667 against 0.883 and 0.867 (p ≤ 0.0005), AgentDyn 0.800 against 1.000 (p = 0.0005), PIDS 0.500
against 0.875 (p = 10⁻⁴). On NotInject, Flash makes fewer false alarms than Gemma26 (0.972 against 0.875, 4 vs 0,
p = 0.125). Windows do not change O61's conclusion that LLaDA2.2 is a weak general detector. They show a possibly
useful long-document niche at about 2–5× the cost of a fast full-text call. That is still much faster than the
comparators: Flash 256/256 on code is about 1.0 s per case, against 3–5 s client time for one Gemma26 or Qwen3.8
call.

## 8. Caveats and the confirmatory design this needs

**Caveats.**

- **Multiple comparisons.** About 3,900 cells, post-hoc selection of the best configs, and mini configs chosen after
  seeing the Flash results on the same cases.
- **Small samples.** 40 long-document cases (20 attacks) per domain.
- **Payload position.** Every LongPIBench payload is at the end of the document (conclusion or last), so tails and
  short remainder windows are confounded with payload position.
- **Templates.** One fixed attack sentence per template per domain, so the "naive" template drives the gains (O2).
- **Long benign controls.** Only 20 per domain, which is too few to rule out a false-alarm rate of a few percent.
- **Single build.** One quantized build per model and one decoder setting.

**Results that need a pre-registered confirmatory run before they are cited as findings:**

1. the Flash long-document gain at 256/256 and 128/128 (tail-free);
2. "zero added false alarms at 256 words or more";
3. parity with Gemma26 and Qwen3.8 on long code and email;
4. any small-window gain on PIDS or BIPIA;
5. any non-max aggregation rule;
6. mini's non-transfer.

**Proposed confirmatory design (F10).**

- **Configs.** Pre-register Flash 4-bit with 256/256 (primary) and 128/128 (secondary), both tail-free by
  construction, plus the full-then-windows cascade.
- **Rule and policy.** max > 0.5, with ignore-invalid as the pre-declared abstention policy and strict reported
  alongside.
- **Cases.** Fresh cases disjoint from the pilot and from this sweep:
  - for each of paper, code and email, ≥ 100 attacks and ≥ 100 clean documents of the same lengths;
  - payloads at the start, middle and end;
  - at least two new subtle templates per domain (the varied-template cohorts).
- **Primary endpoint.** Paired exact McNemar against full text per domain, Holm-corrected over 3.
- **Co-primary safety endpoint.** FPR difference on the clean documents, with a non-inferiority margin of 2 points.
- **Secondary.** Short-content non-inferiority on web and AgentDyn FPR, and mini 6-bit at 512/384 only.
- **Exploratory only.** Thresholds and rules.
